## Assessment 1: Video Assignment

**Due Date**: 26 August 2026

*100% of module*

> Create a video explaining how society has been influenced by earth science in an area of your choice

**Guideline duration:** Minimum 10 minutes of content (i.e. 10min does not include references, credits etc.). There is no specified maximum duration.


You will need to upload the video to Panopto before submitting the assignment - instructions on the assignment page on Brightspace.


**Excluded areas:** The Burren and The Cliffs of Moher are specifically excluded as areas for this assignment.



**Your video should cover:**

1. Where your area is
    - The geology and geological history of the area, including:
    - The plate tectonic settings of the area through it's geological history
    - How the different rocks of the area formed, considering the tectonic settings and environments (i.e., describe and interpret the rocks)
    - Events of note in the geological history of the area
    - Reference to geological map(s) of the area
    - Reference to the geological time scale

2. The influence on society, including:
    - What aspect(s) of society have been influenced by the earth science
    - Which specific earth science factors have influenced the chosen aspect(s) of society
    - How the earth science factor(s) influenced the chosen aspect(s) of society (i.e. how might society be (or have been) different if the geology or geological history of the area had been different)? 

Your video should be based on sources of information from both earth science and human geography.

**Creating a video:**

There are many easy to use free apps and software packages for video creation, across all platforms. Some examples you may use:

- [CapCut](https://www.capcut.com/) (Android, iPhone, iPad, Windows, Mac, web-based)
- [OpenShot](https://www.openshot.org/) (Windows, Mac)
- [iMovie](https://support.apple.com/imovie) (Mac, iPhone, iPad)
- [Microsoft Clipchamp](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/create-films-with-a-video-editor-94e651f8-a5be-ae03-3c50-e49f013d47f6) (web-based, so any platform)
- [Shotcut](https://shotcut.org/) (Windows, Mac)
- [DaVinci Resolve](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve) (Windows, Mac)
- [Kdenlive](https://kdenlive.org/) (Windows, Mac)

PowerPoint does allow recording of presentations, but I strongly recommend against this option.

Your first step, and the majority of the work involved in this assessment, should be choosing your area to cover, and learning about it. If you’re not sure which area to choose, do some reading about various places until you find one that is interesting to you. Read about your chosen area until you are confident you understand the tectonics, rocks, and how society has been influenced by earth science there.

As you read and learn about your chosen area, you will note factors which are, or have been, important; and which you decide will need to be discussed in your video. Compile a list of everything you want to cover. Ensure the list covers all the factors above; add to the list until it does.

Create an outline by putting the list in an order which makes sense. There’s no single right way to do this; it should start with a description of where the area is, but after that it doesn't really matter what order you cover things in, as long as you cover them. You could start by describing the earth science and then link that to society in the area; or you could start with a description of society in the area, and then refer back to the earth science to explain it. There are wrong ways to do it though – try to have reasons for the order of topics.

Once you know what you want to cover, start compiling video footage and images which can illustrate what you want to show or say. This will of course include visuals of the landscapes, rocks, and society of the chosen area, but can also include images or video of particular geological features or processes from other places, and anything else relevant which helps you explain anything in the video. Do leave yourself enough time for this – you’ll need more than you think; the average duration of a single shot in TV and film is around 2 seconds. That doesn’t have to be the case for you; you’re making a video to explain earth science and society in a particular area, not a new movie for Netflix! I’ve certainly made quite a few long single-take videos for students, some more than 10 minutes long without a single cut. But I think you’ll find you want more shorter clips than you might think at first, because that’s what you’re used to watching, and so that’s what you’ll naturally tend to want to make things like. Don’t forget to keep a list of your sources, so that you can credit them.

Of course you can record video yourself – if you choose an area you’ll be able to visit, absolutely take your own footage. You can also record yourself to camera. However, you are not required to do either of these: you do not need to appear in the video yourself if you do not want to.

As you’re compiling your media, you can be adding it to your video project using your chosen video editor. You will almost certainly need to add some voiceover; all the options listed above should allow that easily, by recording an audio file and simply adding it to the project. There are plenty of apps for that too – most phones have a sound recorder app installed by default. If you use generative AI for a voiceover (honestly, discouraged for ethical reasons), cite the model and the prompt, and provide the script input.

While production quality is not a factor, if you want to include music, you can find [multiple sources of legal music here](https://creativecommons.org/legalmusicforvideos/). Again, if you use generative AI for background music (again, discouraged for ethical reasons), cite the model and the prompt.

If you want to get some inspiration on making the video, there’s so many examples on YouTube of people explaining something from their own area of expertise. I’m a bit hesitant to list specific examples, because many are created by people with high-quality cameras, microphones, and other equipment, as well as professional audio and video producers working on the published videos. Yours does not have to be that good. But you could do a lot worse than watch some David Attenborough videos to get you in the right mindset.

**Grading Criteria:**
To attain a passing grade your submission must demonstrate achievement of the module learning outcomes:

1. Explain the process of plate tectonics, and the formation of rocks in different tectonic settings and environments
2. Describe and identify various geological materials, using standard Earth science techniques and terminology, and interpret their origins
3. Use the principles of stratigraphy, the geologic time scale, and information from geological maps and other sources to describe the geological history of an area
4. Relate patterns in human geography to causal geological processes and features
5. Integrate information from disparate sources in earth science and human geography

Your grade will be determined on the basis of the Geography generic marking criteria below, and the following specific criteria:

1. Your choice of area
Your chosen area should give you scope to demonstrate your achievement of the learning outcomes; i.e. to show how much you understand of the earth science of the area, and of how this influenced society in the area. 

You might think you're making it easier on yourself if you choose an area with a single rock type and a simple geological history, because there's less to explain - but that also means you won't be able to demonstrate achievement of the module learning outcomes. That would make it very difficult to pass the assignment, and would also make for a very boring video.

Choose an area with at least a bit of complexity - a few different rock types, and some differences in the tectonic history. That gives you somthing to talk about.

2. Your explanation of the specified factors (i.e. achievement of the learning outcomes).
This is the degree to which you satisfactorily explain the tectonics, rocks, and impact on society, as outlined above.

You will, of course, have to be somewhat selective in your approach: many thousands of words have been written on many different areas, and you cannot cover everything.

Too narrow a focus (e.g. spending two thirds of the video discussing the plate tectonic origin of an area) is as bad as too broad a focus (e.g. considering so many aspects of society which have been influence by the earth science that you can’t spend much discussion on any single one).

3. Your selection of sources of information.
Your information should rely primarily on academic and geographic sources: textbooks, reports, maps, official publications from relevant bodies (e.g. Geological Survey of Ireland), and the academic literature (journal papers). A minimum of 10 academic and geographic sources is expected. You may use other sources as additional references, but with caution: random websites are not reliable.

4. Visual elements.
A video is expected to be strongly visual, illustrating the rocks, landscapes, and features under discussing with video footage and photographs. You may use both your own media and media from other sources, but you must cite the sources of any media which is not your own. If you use generative AI to produce any video, the citation must include both the model and the prompt used.

Note that the video production quality is not a significant factor. I want you to spend the time learning about and explaining the earth science and society in the area, not working out how to do a cross-fade or whatever.
